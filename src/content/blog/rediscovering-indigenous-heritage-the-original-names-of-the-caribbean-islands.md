## The Indigenous Peoples of the Caribbean

Before European colonization in the late 15th century, the Caribbean was home to diverse **Indigenous cultures**, namely the Taíno, Arawak, and Carib peoples. These groups navigated the lush surroundings and were adept at utilizing the available resources for sustenance, trade, and community well-being. Each Indigenous name carries an echo of stories, landscapes, and beliefs, an invaluable representation of a way of life that thrived long before Christopher Columbus arrived.

## The Importance of Indigenous Names

The original names offered by the Indigenous peoples are not merely labels for islands; they embody the history, mentalities, and connection to the land that these cultures held sacred. Each name often corresponds to significant elements of the island's geography, fauna, and local resources, providing insights into how these communities lived in harmony with nature.

## Understanding the Names of Specific Islands

Let's delve deeper into the Indigenous names granted to some of the Caribbean islands and explore their meanings.

### Anguilla

The Indigenous name for Anguilla is **Malliouhana**, translating to "arrow-shaped sea serpent" in the Arawak language. This name reflects the island's elongated shape and is a testament to the rich mythology of the Indigenous peoples.

### Antigua and Barbuda

For Antigua, the indigenous name is **Waladli** or **Wadadli**, signifying "fish oil island" in Kalinago. Barbuda's name, **Wa’omoni**, means "land of the herons" or broadly "land of the large birds." Such names underline the significance of the avian community and fishing practices in the lives of the Indigenous inhabitants.

### Aruba

The name for Aruba, **Oruba**, means "well-situated island" in the Caiquetio language. The alternative Kalinago names for Aruba include **Ora Oubao** meaning "shell island" and **Oirubae** meaning "companion of Curaçao," showcasing the interconnectedness between these islands.

### The Bahamas

The original inhabitants of The Bahamas, the Lucayans, gave names to the major islands reflecting their lifestyle. **Nema**, which means "little waters," is the Taíno name for New Providence. Other names such as **Bahama** for Grand Bahama and **Guanahaní** for San Salvador underscore the nuanced relationship with water and geography.

## Colonization and Name Changes

The arrival of European colonizers dramatically altered the original names and narratives of these Caribbean islands. Explorers like Columbus ignored the rich heritage and renamed these lands after saints, noblemen, or places back in Europe. The erasure of Indigenous names was an attempt to impose a new identity that disregarded the island's original inhabitants and their histories.

On **Puerto Rico**, the indigenous name "**Borinquen**," meaning "**land of the valiant and noble lord**," carries a sense of pride, representing the strength and resilience of its original inhabitants.
